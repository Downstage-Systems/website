Set up a light.

The same four steps for the Downstage Cue, the Cue Lite and the Cue 360. It is also on the card in the box.

  1. Power it

    Plug in USB-C. A new light opens its own setup network, named for the light and its four-character id, like Downstage Cue 360 66B8. The password is downstage.

  2. Join it

    Join that network from your phone. The setup page opens by itself; if it does not, go to http://192.168.4.1/.

  3. Put it on your network

    Pick your WiFi and enter its password. Under Companion, enter the Companion computer's address and the Satellite port, 16622. Save.

  4. Give it a button

    In Companion's Surfaces page, set the light's page and position so its key sits on the button it should follow. Red is on air, green is preview.

A Cue 360 can skip WiFi: hold it to a Downstage Cue or a Cue Lite for two seconds and it links over CueLink.

Questions.

Do I need a Downstage One to use the Cue lights?
No. Every Cue light works with any Bitfocus Companion over WiFi, on its own. The Downstage One, coming later, adds the show clock and gives Companion a home in your Cue system.
What is CueLink?
CueLink is the lights' own radio. Hold two lights together for two seconds and they link: from then on the Cue carries the other light to Companion, so the linked light needs no WiFi where it stands. A Cue 360 follows its Cue's tally until you give it its own Companion button. A Downstage Cue carries up to four lights and a Cue Lite carries one. Lights in your Cue system sign what they send, so the system in the next room cannot drive yours.
How do they mount?
The Downstage Cue and the Cue 360 have magnets in their backs for steel and a 1/4-20 for an arm or a shoe. The Cue Lite takes a 1/4-20, and a hot shoe adapter comes in the box with both screens.
What powers them?
USB-C, on all three: a USB-C charger or a power bank. Some camera USB ports cannot supply enough power, so check yours before the show. USB power is how the lights run a show. The Downstage Cue and the Cue Lite have a built-in backup battery, about 2 hours and about 1 h 20 min away from power; no light lasts a show day on battery. The Cue 360 runs on USB power only.
Is the software open?
Downstage OS, which runs the One, is public on GitHub. It stands on Ontime, free and open source under GPL v3 (source), and Bitfocus Companion. We publish our manuals before you buy.

Troubleshooting.

A Downstage light says what is wrong in a sentence. Here is what to do about it.

The setup page did not open
Stay joined to the light's own network and go to http://192.168.4.1/ in your phone's browser.
It says the WiFi has a sign-in page
Hotel and venue networks that want a login in a browser cannot carry tally: a light cannot get past the sign-in page. Use a network without one, your own router, or a phone hotspot. A Cue 360 has no screen to say it: it opens its own setup network beside the venue's, and a magenta dot circles its edge. Join that network from your phone and pick another WiFi.
It is on WiFi, but cannot reach Companion
Guest networks often keep devices apart. Use a network where devices can see each other, check the Companion address and port 16622 on the light's page, and check that Companion's Satellite service is on (it is by default).
It says WEAK WIFI
The light is losing messages on your WiFi, so tally can lag or drop out. It says so when one in ten of its checks goes unanswered for ten seconds, and clears once the link settles. Downstage lights run on 2.4 GHz WiFi, so a strong 2.4 GHz signal where the lights sit matters most. Move the access point or the light closer, keep metal, walls and crowds out of the line between them, and avoid a busy guest network if you can. The Downstage Cue says it on its rim and the Cue Lite in its top bar, where the clock usually is. A light carried over CueLink says WEAK CUELINK when its link is dropping messages: bring it closer to its Relay or to the light it links through.
Does it work on 5 GHz WiFi?
No. Downstage lights join 2.4 GHz networks only, which reach further through walls and crowds than 5 GHz. Most venue and home routers offer both under one name and the light picks the 2.4 GHz side by itself. If yours is 5 GHz only, turn on its 2.4 GHz band or use another router. For the steadiest tally, use a strong network of your own rather than a crowded guest one.
It says the password is wrong
Join the light's setup network again and re-enter the WiFi password.
I moved to a new venue
When a light cannot find its saved WiFi, it opens its setup network by itself after about a minute. Join it and pick the new network. It keeps its name and number.
Why does my screen shift around?
On purpose. The Downstage Cue and the Cue Lite have AMOLED screens, and a picture that never moves, like a camera number left up all day, can burn into an OLED screen. To prevent that, the light moves its picture a few pixels every few minutes. It is too small to notice from where you stand, and it protects the screen.
Why doesn't my Cue or Lite show the time?
A light has no way to know the time of day by itself: it has to be told. The Downstage Cue and the Cue Lite set their clock from the internet, or from a Downstage One on the same network. So the time shows only when the light has an active internet connection or can see a One. On a closed show network with neither, the light leaves the clock off rather than show the wrong time.
How do I reset a light?
Open the light's page and choose Factory reset. It wipes every saved network, the name and all settings, and the light comes back as new.
How do I update a light?
Updates are uploaded from the light's own page, and the light restarts on the new version. Firmware downloads will be posted on this page.

Still stuck?

Email us with what the light says and what you were doing. A photo of its screen helps.
